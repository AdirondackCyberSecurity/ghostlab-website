GhostLab: Demon Box Privacy Policy

Effective date: November 20, 2026 · Applies to the iOS and Android apps “GhostLab: Demon Box”

GhostLab: Demon Box is a paranormal investigative tool. During a hunt it records the room, reads your phone’s sensors and shows words in a ring of runes, and voices on your phone speak them aloud. You decide what the words mean. Demon Box has no accounts, no ads and no servers of our own. Your hunts stay on your phone unless you choose to share them. The app counts how it is used through Google Analytics for Firebase, described under Usage statistics.

Summary

  • Usage statistics. The app counts how it is used through Google Analytics for Firebase. It never sends your recordings, the words in your hunts, anything you say or type, your name, or your GPS location. In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom, and Switzerland it sends none at all. There is no crash reporting service, no advertising and no tracking.
  • No accounts and no sign-in.
  • Your hunts stay on your phone. Recordings, word logs and sensor readings leave the app only when you share them or make a backup.
  • The microphone records the room during a hunt.
  • Speech recognition runs only on your phone. If your phone cannot recognize speech on the device, listening stays off.
  • Location is your choice. When you allow it, each hunt saves where it was recorded, and your phone’s map service is asked what kind of place you are in. See Location.
  • The camera is never used.
  • One unlock. Demon Box is a free download. The first 15 words in your hunts are free, and one purchase in the App Store or Google Play unlocks it for good. See Purchases.

The microphone

While a hunt runs, Demon Box records the room through the microphone and saves the recording on your phone. The recording holds the sound of the room, your voice and the words the voices speak. The microphone also listens for knocks and raps during the hunt. The app asks for microphone access when you start your first hunt, and a hunt cannot start without it.

Speech recognition

In Sensor + Voice, Demon Box listens for the questions you ask so they can steer the words. Recognition runs only on your phone. On iPhone it uses Apple’s on-device speech recognition, and the app asks for speech recognition access when you start your first hunt. On Android it uses Android’s on-device recognizer, which needs Android 13 or later. When your phone cannot recognize your chosen language on the device, listening stays off and nothing is sent anywhere. In Sensor mode the app does not listen for questions at all.

The questions it hears are saved as text in that hunt’s log on your phone. If you say your name during a hunt, Demon Box remembers it on your phone so the voice can use it in a later hunt. Deleting the app removes it.

The sensors

During a hunt Demon Box reads the magnetometer, the barometer, the motion sensors and the compass. The readings are used live on the phone. Each hunt saves a timeline of the magnetic readings and the direction the phone faced. On iPhone, the Motion & Fitness permission lets the app read air pressure and tell whether you are walking, driving or climbing stairs, so your own movement is never taken for a knock or a change in the room. That activity reading is used live and is not saved with the hunt.

Location

Location is optional, and hunts run without it.

  • When it is asked: on iPhone, Demon Box asks to use your location while the app is open when its hunt screen first appears. On Android it asks only when you tap Allow location in Settings. You can turn location off for the app at any time in your phone’s settings.
  • Where each hunt was recorded: when location is allowed, the best position the phone finds during a hunt is saved with that hunt as coordinates, on your phone. A hunt you share leaves the coordinates out. A Library backup keeps them.
  • The kind of place you are in: when a hunt starts and location is allowed, Demon Box sends your current position to your phone’s map service to learn what is nearby, such as a cemetery, a church, woods or water, so the place can shape the words. On iPhone this is Apple’s geocoding service. On Android it is the phone’s geocoder, which is usually provided by Google. The app turns the answer into a few words about that kind of place for the hunt. The address that comes back is not saved, and we never receive your position.
  • Telling a car from a room: in Sensor mode, your speed lets the app tell a moving car from a still room. Speed is used live and is not saved.
  • Earth’s magnetic field on Android: the Android app also uses your position to work out, on the phone, how strong Earth’s magnetic field is where you are, which helps the magnetometer judge its readings.

The voices

The words are spoken by voices that run on your phone. Demon Box’s own voices need no internet connection. On Android, the voice engine’s built-in usage reporting to Microsoft is removed from the app and switched off.

Usage statistics

GhostLab: Demon Box uses Google Analytics for Firebase on iPhone and Android to count how the app is used, so we can see how many people start a hunt, which parts of the app they use, and where they stop.

It records:

  • What you do in the app, in general terms: finishing or skipping the tutorial and the page you left it on, starting a hunt and whether it used Sensor or Sensor + Voice, saving a hunt, how long it ran and roughly how many words were spoken in it (as a range, never the words), opening the share sheet and the kind of item (a clip, a recording, the hunt’s text or a backup), the app you shared to when it is a well-known one such as TikTok or YouTube, opening the Guide, the voice and the language pack you pick, when the app asks your phone to show the rating card, opening the unlock screen and where you opened it from, using your last free word, and buying the unlock.
  • When the unlock is bought, Firebase also notes the purchase itself: the product, its price and the currency. It never receives your payment details or your Apple or Google account.
  • An app-instance identifier that Firebase creates for Demon Box on your phone. It is not linked to your name, your Apple or Google account, or your phone’s advertising identifier. It resets if you delete the app, or on Android if you clear its data.
  • Basic device and app details: phone model, iOS or Android version, app version, language, and country.
  • Your approximate area, such as your city and country, which Google works out from your IP address. Google Analytics does not store the IP address.

It never records your recordings or clips, the words shown or spoken in a hunt, the questions you ask, the name you say aloud, your custom words, anything you type, session names and notes, or your location from GPS. Demon Box does not collect your phone’s advertising identifier. On Android the permission is removed from the app and collection is switched off. The iPhone app never asks to track you and leaves out the part of Firebase that can read the advertising identifier, and it does not collect the iPhone’s vendor identifier either.

In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom, and Switzerland, the app does not send usage statistics on iPhone or Android.

Google processes this information for us as a service provider. We have turned off Google’s options to use it for its own products or for advertising, and we do not link it to Google Ads. The recorded actions are kept for 2 months, and the identifier until 2 months after you last use the app. Then Google deletes them automatically. See Data deletion for what that means for a deletion request.

Data the app keeps on your device

  • Hunts: the recording; the log of words, marks and the questions it heard; the magnetic timeline; the direction the phone faced; where the hunt was recorded, when location is allowed; any notes you add; and technical details about the recording such as the phone model, its system version and the microphone used. Your hunts are listed in the Library.
  • Clips: screen videos of a hunt with its sound, kept with that hunt.
  • Settings: your custom words and names, the words to leave out, your language pack, voice and other choices, the name you said aloud, and a short list of words already spoken so they do not come back too soon.

On iPhone these files live in the app’s own storage. They do not appear in the Files app, and like other app data they are included in your iCloud or computer backup when backups are turned on. On Android they live in the app’s private storage, and the app opts out of Android’s backup to your Google account. Android’s phone-to-phone transfer may still copy them when you set up a new phone.

Backups you make

Settings can build one ZIP file of your whole Library, with each hunt’s audio, marks, notes and precise location, and then opens your phone’s share sheet so you choose where to keep it. Only give a backup to people you trust with those places. On iPhone you can also restore a backup into the Library from Settings.

Deleting your data

You can delete any hunt in the Library. On iPhone you can also select several hunts, or all of them, and delete them together. On Android, Settings has Delete all hunts, which removes every hunt and its recording from the phone. Deleting the app removes everything it stored on your phone, including your settings and the name it remembered. On iPhone, a device backup made earlier may still hold a copy.

Sharing

Nothing leaves the app unless you send it. When you share a recording, a clip or a hunt, your phone’s share sheet sends it to the app or person you choose. On iPhone, sharing a clip to Facebook, Instagram, TikTok or YouTube first saves it to your Photos library, which the app asks permission for, and then opens that app so you can post it. Once something leaves Demon Box, the app or person you shared it with decides what happens to it.

Purchases

Demon Box is a free download. The first 15 words in your hunts are free. After that, starting a hunt needs the one-time unlock, bought in the App Store or Google Play. There is no subscription and no ads, and the Library stays open whether or not you buy. If you bought Demon Box on the App Store when it was a paid download, you keep everything without buying again.

Apple or Google handles the payment, and we receive no payment details and nothing that identifies you. The app asks the store on your phone whether the unlock is owned, so it can open Demon Box and bring the unlock back with Restore Purchase.

So that deleting and reinstalling the app does not start the free words again, the app keeps the count of free words used on your phone: on iPhone in the Keychain, and on Android in the app's own storage with a copy in Google's Block Store, which Android keeps with your Google account's backup. The count holds only a number.

Ratings

Now and then Demon Box may show Apple’s or Google’s own rating card. Apple or Google handles your rating. On Android, Settings also has a link that opens Demon Box on Google Play.

Internet use

Demon Box has no servers of its own. Apart from the usage statistics described under Usage statistics, the place lookup described under Location and the stores’ own rating cards, the app does not go online. Links in Settings, such as this policy, open in your browser. The Android app lists Android’s internet permission because a part it shares with the GhostLab Toolkit needs that permission to start. Demon Box does not use it to send your hunts, recordings or settings anywhere.

What we do not do

  • We do not sell personal data.
  • We do not run advertising or tracking tools in the app. The app’s only analytics is the usage statistics described above.
  • We do not upload your hunts, recordings, words, questions or location.
  • We do not ask you to sign up or give an email address.

Children

The app carries the age rating shown on its App Store and Google Play pages. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children, and the app collects no personal information from anyone. The app’s usage statistics are described above.

Changes

If our practices change, we will update this page and the effective date above before the change takes effect.
