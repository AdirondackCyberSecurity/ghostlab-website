Filing a notice

Your notice must be in writing and include:

  • Your physical or electronic signature, or that of someone authorised to act for the owner.
  • The copyrighted work you believe was infringed.
  • The link to each gallery item you want removed, so we can find it.
  • Your name, mailing address, phone number and email.
  • A statement that you believe in good faith the use is not authorised by the owner, its agent or the law.
  • A statement that the information in your notice is accurate and, under penalty of perjury, that you are the owner or authorised to act for the owner.

When a notice is complete, we remove or disable the item promptly and let the person who submitted it know.

Counter-notices

If your submission was removed and you believe that was a mistake or misidentification, you can send our agent a counter-notice containing:

  • Your physical or electronic signature.
  • The item that was removed and the link where it appeared.
  • A statement under penalty of perjury that you believe in good faith it was removed by mistake or misidentification.
  • Your name, address and phone number, and a statement that you consent to the jurisdiction of the federal district court for your address (or, outside the US, any district where we may be found), and that you will accept service of process from the person who sent the original notice.

We forward a complete counter-notice to the person who sent the original notice. Unless they tell us within 10 business days that they have filed a court action, we may restore the item 10 to 14 business days after we received the counter-notice.

Repeat infringers

We keep a record of valid notices. Anyone who receives three valid notices that are not successfully countered within twelve months loses the ability to submit to the gallery, and we may act sooner in serious cases.

Other removal requests

If something in the gallery shows you, your home or your property and you want it removed, you don’t need a copyright notice. Email ghostlabsupport@inkboxmail.com and we will look at it quickly.

Sending a notice or counter-notice that knowingly misrepresents the facts can make you liable for damages under 17 U.S.C. § 512(f).
